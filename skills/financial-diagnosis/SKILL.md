# Financial Diagnosis

## Core Workflow

1. Clarify the decision context: turnaround, growth funding, margin improvement, M&A diligence, budget review, business unit diagnosis, or board reporting.
2. Inventory available data: income statement, balance sheet, cash flow, management accounts, product/customer/channel data, budgets, peer benchmarks, and time period coverage.
3. Normalize the data before judging it: currency, fiscal period, one-offs, accounting policy changes, discontinued operations, related-party items, and consolidation scope.
4. Build the issue tree from value creation drivers: growth, gross margin, operating leverage, asset efficiency, capital structure, cash conversion, and risk.
5. Quantify the movement: level, trend, mix, price/volume, bridge, variance, contribution, sensitivity, and peer gap.
6. State a concrete finding from each comparison: identify the company, metric, period, quantified gap, and business consequence. Attribute root causes only when supported by additional evidence. Add management actions when the assignment requests them; a diagnosis or case study need not contain recommendations.
7. State evidence gaps explicitly instead of filling them with speculation.

## Output Standard

Select only the sections that serve the assignment. User instructions to omit recommendations govern the whole report, including captions and conclusions. A diagnostic case study can end with findings. Do not automatically append management actions, generic lessons, or unverified caveats. Keep unresolved evidence questions in a separate working log when the requested formal report admits only verified findings.

Useful structures include:

- Executive diagnosis: 3-5 numbered findings, each with a financial fact and management implication.
- Driver tree: show how revenue, margin, cash, ROIC, or enterprise value decomposes.
- Evidence table: metric, current value, prior value, benchmark, variance, likely driver, required validation.
- Management actions: quick wins, structural initiatives, owner, KPI, estimated impact, timing, dependencies.
- Risks and caveats: accounting comparability, data quality, one-off events, market cycle, customer concentration.

Use precise language. Replace "performance is poor" with "gross margin fell 4.2 pp YoY, mainly from product mix shift and input cost lag, compressing EBITDA by approximately RMB 38m before mitigation."

## Guardrails

- Do not present investment, legal, audit, tax, or accounting advice as definitive professional advice.
- Do not infer fraud from data alone; phrase concerns as anomalies requiring validation.
- Do not mix audited statements and management accounts without marking scope differences.
- If data is missing, produce a "minimum viable analysis" and list the data needed for a stronger conclusion.

## Peer Comparisons and Recent Events

- Calculate peer gaps on named, reconciled metrics before writing conclusions. Give the actual result of the comparison; phrases such as the value of benchmarking is joint evaluation do not state what happened or why it matters.
- Reconcile the numerator and denominator of every derived ratio at business-unit level. Manufacturing revenue that includes commercial vehicles cannot silently be divided by passenger-car sales. Distinguish a company's earlier preliminary unit metric from a later audited segment-based calculation; label the chosen basis and use it consistently.
- For R&D, distinguish total investment, current expense, newly capitalized development costs, transfers to intangible assets, amortization and impairment. Give formulas, scope and fiscal year. Compare sales, product mix, margins and cash outcomes alongside inputs; a one-year input/output ratio alone does not establish R&D causality. Do not call capitalization a cash return or treat capitalization rate alone as proof of misconduct.
- When numerical peer comparison drives the finding, use a readable chart or aligned table with the figures, units and exact sources. Distinguish absolute amounts from ratios, annual from interim data, and production/wholesale/retail/deliveries. Draw the conclusion beside the evidence, rather than burying it in generic management advice.
- Refresh material company events through the report cutoff date. Verify entity, transaction stage, product timing and measured market feedback using primary disclosures. An announced transaction is not completed integration; a controversy at another partner brand does not establish a loss at the subject company. Cite the precise fact immediately after the supported clause and retain the primary document in the evidence log.
