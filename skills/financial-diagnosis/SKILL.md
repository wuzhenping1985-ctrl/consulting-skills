# Financial Diagnosis

## Core Workflow

1. Clarify the decision context: turnaround, growth funding, margin improvement, M&A diligence, budget review, business unit diagnosis, or board reporting.
2. Inventory available data: income statement, balance sheet, cash flow, management accounts, product/customer/channel data, budgets, peer benchmarks, and time period coverage.
3. Normalize the data before judging it: currency, fiscal period, one-offs, accounting policy changes, discontinued operations, related-party items, and consolidation scope.
4. Build the issue tree from value creation drivers: growth, gross margin, operating leverage, asset efficiency, capital structure, cash conversion, and risk.
5. Quantify the movement: level, trend, mix, price/volume, bridge, variance, contribution, sensitivity, and peer gap.
6. Convert observations into findings: "so what", root cause, business implication, urgency, confidence level, and management action.
7. State evidence gaps explicitly instead of filling them with speculation.

## Output Standard

Prefer consulting-grade structure:

- Executive diagnosis: 3-5 numbered findings, each with a financial fact and management implication.
- Driver tree: show how revenue, margin, cash, ROIC, or enterprise value decomposes.
- Evidence table: metric, current value, prior value, benchmark, variance, likely driver, required validation.
- Management actions: quick wins, structural initiatives, owner, KPI, estimated impact, timing, dependencies.
- Risks and caveats: accounting comparability, data quality, one-off events, market cycle, customer concentration.

Use precise language. Replace "performance is poor" with "gross margin fell 4.2 pp YoY, mainly from product mix shift and input cost lag, compressing EBITDA by approximately RMB 38m before mitigation."

## Guardrails

- Do not present investment, legal, audit, tax, or accounting advice as definitive professional advice.
- Do not infer fraud from data alone; phrase concerns as anomalies requiring validation.
- Do not mix audited statements and management accounts without marking scope differences.
- If data is missing, produce a "minimum viable analysis" and list the data needed for a stronger conclusion.
