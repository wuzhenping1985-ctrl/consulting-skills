# Company Case Analysis

Use this mode for explaining a company's performance change or strategic transformation. A chronology of news, figures followed by generic implications, and a list of management initiatives do not establish the cause of the result.

For each main finding, connect the observed change to a specific business mechanism and its measured consequence. Distinguish what the source states, what the calculation shows, and what the analyst infers. Retain that distinction in the evidence record; formal prose can present the supported conclusion directly. If the missing mechanism cannot be verified, narrow the conclusion instead of inventing a cause.

Useful causal checks:

- Sales: identify which products, customers, channels or markets account for the change; distinguish replacement cycles from recurring demand and model launches from sustained sales.
- Profit: separate volume, price or mix, unit cost, fixed-cost absorption, operating expenses and one-offs. A numerical bridge locates the movement; it does not prove every unit-cost change came from idle capacity. Avoid counting the same volume effect again through fixed-cost absorption.
- Cash: distinguish profit, non-cash expenses, working capital, investment and financing. Impairment reduces profit but does not itself create the same-period cash outflow. Keep group, segment and financial-subsidiary scopes aligned.
- R&D: connect project readiness, amortization policy, economic life, forecast sales and pricing, and impairment evidence. Normal capitalization or transfer to intangibles is not commercial success or failure. Do not divide group technical-asset amortization by a narrower segment's volume and call it that segment's unit cost.
- Transactions: explain the acquired earnings source, strategic concentration, resource-sharing mechanism, consideration and dilution, dividends and financing separately. Announced synergies remain proposed; verify each direction of financing conditions. A profitable target is not proof of value creation at an unknown purchase price.
- Current events: use entity, timing and measured business linkage. A partner's other-brand controversy cannot be inserted as a cause of the subject company's loss without a verified transmission path. Keep independently relevant context separate from the causal findings.

Benchmark an operating mechanism rather than merely a rival's size. Show comparable measures and concrete product or operating results. Shared architecture plus high sales supports product scale; claims of measured cost savings require cost evidence. Update the analysis when recent figures reverse an older trend.

Before delivery, read the finding without its figures. It should still name what changed in the business and how that affected results. Then read the evidence without the finding: it must support the exact entity, period, scope and strength of the conclusion. Remove any recommendation that the assignment excludes, including recommendations disguised as lessons or management value.


## Explaining Why One Company Underperformed

A lower volume, higher unit cost or impairment charge locates the loss; it does not explain the preceding business cause. Trace the chain back to a verified product choice, customer dependency, decision process, resource arrangement or capability gap, then explain how that changed the offer, customer access, lead time or cost and finally the observed result. Contemporaneous company disclosures of concrete operating problems are stronger than reversing a later reform announcement into an assumed historical failure.

Use matched product, market and period comparisons where available. Record launch dates, price basis, powertrain and wholesale versus retail scope. A rival selling more establishes a gap; product specifications alone do not establish why buyers preferred it. Combine the comparison with verified customer, channel or development evidence, and do not invent causal shares.

Test the explanation against the same company's later improvement. Identify which product, process or market changed and whether results changed with it; keep simultaneous export growth, channel changes and other drivers visible. Assess investment decisions against demand and information available at the time: an initially justified expansion can become a burden when demand reverses before products and assets adjust. Distinguish the original decision from the subsequent response.
