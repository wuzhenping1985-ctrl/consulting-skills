# Industry Research

## Core Workflow

1. Define the industry boundary: product/service scope, geography, value chain position, customer segment, and time horizon.
2. Separate facts, estimates, and hypotheses. Mark source dates and confidence when using external research.
3. Build the industry logic: demand drivers, supply structure, value chain economics, policy constraints, technology path, capital cycle, and profit pool.
4. Analyze structure before players: market size, growth, concentration, bargaining power, entry barriers, substitution, and bottlenecks.
5. Translate industry findings into strategic implications: where to play, how to win, capabilities required, risks, and timing.
6. Produce a source-backed research note or deck-ready storyline.

## Output Standard

For most industry work, use:

- One-paragraph executive view: industry attractiveness, inflection point, and strategic implication.
- Market definition and sizing logic.
- Value chain and profit pool.
- Demand drivers and customer segmentation.
- Supply structure and competitive landscape.
- Policy, regulation, and technology trends.
- Scenario outlook and uncertainties.
- Implications for the client: opportunities, threats, capabilities, and next moves.

## Guardrails

- Browse or use current sources when the answer depends on market data, policy, prices, regulation, company status, or recent events.
- Do not cite unsupported numbers. If using assumptions, show the equation and sensitivity.
- Do not confuse "large market" with "attractive market"; attractiveness requires profit pool, barriers, timing, and fit.
- Keep Chinese policy and regional planning analysis explicit about document date, issuing authority, and implementation uncertainty.
